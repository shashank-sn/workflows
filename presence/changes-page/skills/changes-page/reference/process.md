# Process notes

## Order of the full channel page

1. **Title** and top lines: why it matters, what is wrong today, what we change, what it takes, places this page covers, state with a date.
2. **Decisions.** A table: decision, who decides, our recommendation, what waits on it. With none, write "No decisions."
3. **To-dos.** A table: what needs to be done, done when. In the order the work happens. A to-do that retires old copies comes before anything is sent.
4. **Before and after.** One line each for today, after the changes, and the best example.
5. **The changes.** One heading per place where a change is made, then one numbered change each (see `change-card.md`).
6. **Strategy.** Start with a verdict: needed, low priority or not needed, with its evidence and a re-check rhythm.
7. **Who reads it.** The reader by job, the question they arrive with, and any measurement with count and date.
8. **The best examples.** Three to five pages, each read signed out and dated, each with the one thing it does well. Then the pattern.
9. **The company today.** What is there now, quoted and dated. What is wrong or missing, and why it matters to the reader.
10. **How we will know it worked.** A table: what is measured, where and how it is read, today, target, when, who checks. Each row is a Check (a thing you can observe) or an Aim (a hope).
11. **Other open items** and **Sources**. Every source linked and dated.

## The verdict

- **needed:** a reader or an assistant meets a wrong, empty or stale page today, and the place matters to them. Give the evidence.
- **low priority:** do it once and check each quarter. Nothing is wrong enough to hurt today.
- **not needed:** the company has no reader there. Give the evidence and write no changes page.

## Who does a change

Name one actor per card.

- **A role at the company** (the CEO, an engineer, the docs admin), when the change needs their account or their judgment.
- **We**, when you make the change with access the company has granted. Say how: "We make this change once you send the invite."
- Never ask for a password. Ask for an invite with the smallest role that works.

## What a decision is

A decision is a choice the sources cannot settle. Quote the text being decided and give your recommendation: "We'd go with X, because Y." Do not ask who signs in or which email to use. Those are access questions you can settle yourself.

## Cold read questions

1. What is the first thing you would do? Is it clear which page to open?
2. Which step could you not finish, and why?
3. Which word did you not know?
4. What did you expect to see that is missing?
5. Does any line sound like an argument against doing the work?

## Counts on a page

Recount every number after each edit. A title that says "three changes" lists three. A time given for a founder adds up from the cards.
