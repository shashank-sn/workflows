# Process notes

## Question rules

- Pick debates from the last day or two. A debate is a live argument, not a topic.
- Ask for the person's sharp opinion on that debate and the experience behind it.
- At most every fifth day, one question may be a follow-up. It quotes an earlier answer and asks for more depth.
- Spread the questions across areas the person writes about, such as relationships, business and culture. Use the areas the person names.

## Two drafts

| Draft | Rule |
|---|---|
| a | Same order, same words. Contract and tighten only. |
| b | Different entry point from the same answer, for example the number first or a quoted line first. |

Both drafts follow the person's house rules for case, punctuation and length. The house rules win over the editor when they conflict.

## The phrase list

List each phrase in a draft that is not in the answer. Quote it, name the draft and the line, and say what the answer said instead. The person cuts what they dislike.

## Editor loop

1. Start a session with the voice profile and the writing brief.
2. Review the draft. If it fails, rewrite from the findings and review again.
3. Stop at a pass, or when the editor reports no more gain.
4. If the editor's own settings conflict with the brief (for example a point-of-view rule), keep the brief and say so in the draft file.
5. Compare the final draft with the answer. Revert any added claim.

## Slots and checks

- Use a small set of fixed daily slots. Find the next free one by listing scheduled posts for the next three days.
- A slot must be at least 15 minutes ahead.
- Images go in by the person, in the scheduler, unless the scheduler accepts a stable public link.
- The 24-hour check reads views or impressions and engagement. Compare against the median of the person's earlier posts from the same source.

## Statuses

`draft` then `approved` then `scheduled` then `published`. A sibling that was not chosen is `dropped`. A question with no answer is `unanswered`.
